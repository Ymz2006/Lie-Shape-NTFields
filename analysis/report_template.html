Metric, Shape and Lie NTFields

What differs

All three use the original metric NTFields network (single-route, Fourier features, Lipschitz layers, τ = smooth-max distance between endpoint embeddings), the same data, 4000 epochs and the same trainer. Only the Eikonal residual changes. S*d, S*a are the warped translational / rotational clearance speeds; S*c is the baseline's warped combined speed.

metric( √(S*c · |∇qτ|) − 1 )² — one isotropic speed for all six coordinates shape( S*d|∇pτ| − 1 )² + ( S*a|∇rτ| − 1 )² — blocked: each block on its own, chart (rotation-vector) gradient lie( √(S*d²|∇vτ|² + S*a²|∇ωτ|²) − 1 )² — anisotropic, body-frame Lie gradient ∇ωτ = Jr−T∇rτ

    Which metric did each field learn

    From probe points, τ is measured for short steps (h = 0.05) that mix translation and body rotation at angle φ (0° = pure translation, 90° = pure rotation). If the two costs add, √2·τ(45°) = τ(0°) + τ(90°) (exponent p = 1, the blocked equation's free-space solution). If they combine in quadrature, p = 2 (the isotropic and anisotropic equations). The right panel shows the start gradient's dual norms as the goal direction turns: the blocked equation predicts (1, 1) throughout, the anisotropic one the quarter circle.

    Dual norms of ∇τ as the goal direction turns

    Rotation, the chart and the cut locus

    Wrap pairs: same position and axis, rotations θ₀ and −θ₁ with θ in [130°, 175°]: 10–100° apart on SO(3), but far apart in rotation-vector coordinates. Each has a control with the same start, axis and geodesic angle on the near side. Cut-locus pairs: one pose written with two rotation vectors (r and r − r/|r|).
